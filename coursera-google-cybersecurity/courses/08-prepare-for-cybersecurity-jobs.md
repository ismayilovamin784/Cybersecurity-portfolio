## Big picture
Technical skills are only part of the job. An entry-level analyst also has to decide when to escalate, explain a problem to people who are not technical, act ethically and keep learning. This course covers those skills.

## Protecting data and handling incidents
- Data and assets are **classified** so the most sensitive ones get the most protection.
- **Disaster recovery** restores systems after a disruption, and **business continuity** keeps the organization working during it.
- **Event and incident detection** means telling the difference between a normal event and a real incident.

## Escalation
**Escalation** means passing an incident to the right person or team. Escalating too late can let damage grow, and escalating wrongly wastes time and attention. The course covers how to recognize when an incident needs to move up, who is responsible at each step and why timing matters.

## Communication
- **Stakeholders** are the people affected by or responsible for a security decision.
- Messages should be clear and short, and fit the audience. A manager needs the impact and the next step, not every technical detail.
- **Visual storytelling** and dashboards can show a trend or a risk faster than a long report.
- Sensitive information has to be shared with care and confidentiality.

## Ethics and the security community
A security professional has access to sensitive data, so an ethical mindset is part of protecting it. Threats and tools change quickly, so the course also covers how to stay current through reliable sources, organizations and communities, and how to connect with other professionals.

## Using AI in security work
AI can help analysts with tasks such as explaining complex frameworks, reviewing code, spotting bugs, analyzing event logs and prioritizing alerts. The result still needs a human check, because an AI tool can be wrong and may not know the full context.

## Job preparation
The course also includes tips for cover letters and technical interviews, and an introduction to Google AI Essentials.

## What I learned
I learned that communication and judgment are as important as tools. Knowing when to escalate, how to explain a risk clearly and how to handle sensitive data responsibly are things employers look for. I also learned that AI is a useful assistant for an analyst, as long as I check its output.
